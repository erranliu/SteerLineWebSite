## About the author

My name is Liu Ming (刘明). I live in Beijing, have a technical background, and have spent 20 years in the games industry. Since 2012, I have started two ventures and raised several rounds of funding. Alongside SteerLine, I also have a small game company that continues to operate steadily.

## A real transition, delivery challenges, and project experience

At the end of 2025, after spending two months learning about AI, I decided to move the game company's entire development team to AI development. Everyone stopped following the traditional development process we had used for ten years and began exploring AI workflows. We had already completed the transition to fully AI-generated art assets in 2023. This time, all programmers, game designers, and artists on the development team left their previous processes behind and began building new workflows with AI.

It did not go smoothly. For a long time, most people struggled to understand what their role should be in the new workflow.

We launched several small projects along the way. But our first medium-sized project to reach commercial delivery, with consistent revenue from paid user acquisition, only made it to launch in June 2026 after considerable difficulty. It contained 400,000 lines of C# code, excluding tests. It was built on an existing project, and even after the AI had finished its work, manual testing and fixes were needed for more than a hundred bugs.

During this transition, I experienced firsthand the problems traditional developers encounter when moving to AI, including misconceptions and entrenched ways of thinking that are difficult to move beyond. Alongside ongoing game development, I also developed several projects built around AI from the outset. Some reached delivery; others were abandoned partway through. I believe I have more hands-on project development experience than most AI developers working over the same period. Projects that are either too large or too small make it difficult to experience the full development and delivery cycle of a commercial project.

SteerLine is a next-generation AI development workflow and toolset designed from real project experience and the latest understanding emerging from the community.

Starting in May 2026, the AI development community's understanding began to settle around Specs and tasks as the core units of development. These are also foundational concepts in SteerLine.

SteerLine does not approach AGI as something simple or easily attainable. We aim to keep evolving the development workflow alongside the community's latest engineering insights, helping every user learn and adopt up-to-date workflows at the lowest possible cost.

Over the long term, we aim first for reliable delivery with AI development, then for greater efficiency and a gradual reduction in human involvement and workload, ultimately progressing toward engineering AGI.

## Why we write these AI development guides

A grounding in basic AI development concepts is essential to using SteerLine well. That is where these guides began.

Fully adopting SteerLine means accepting its conventions for the project workflow. If you prefer not to adopt the whole system, we still want everything in these guides to be useful to you and to contribute to the wider AI industry. You can learn what you need and apply the methods to your own projects and development process.

We also provide **SteerRunner**, a simple parallel task executor that can be used independently. You can use it without fully adopting SteerLine.

## Free, with ongoing updates

**We will keep these SteerLine AI development guides free and continue updating them over the long term.**

We hope they help you understand and use SteerLine, while also serving as methods and experience you can draw on independently in your own AI development work.
